Experiment 02: Dropout

Hypothesis:
- Test whether injecting Dropout layers with a rate of 0.4 between the dense layers 
  prevents neuron co-adaptation and improves generalization on unseen data.

Kept equal to baseline:
- Original, unnormalized image inputs.
- SGD with learning rate 3e-4, momentum=0.9 and nesterov=True.
- Batch size: 16.
- Maximum epochs: 100.
- Same train/validation split and callbacks.

Changed:
- Architecture changed to interleave Dropout layers:
  Flatten -> Dense(512) -> Dropout(0.4) -> Dense(256) -> Dropout(0.4) -> 
  Dense(128) -> Dropout(0.4) -> Dense(64) -> Dropout(0.4) -> Dense(13).
- Checkpoint and prediction paths point to experiment_02.

Challenge compliance:
- Feedforward neural network using dense layers only.
- No CNN, transfer learning, or classical data augmentation.
- USES dropout, which is the focus of this experiment.

Results:
- Best validation model: epoch 82.
- Training stopped at epoch 100 (maximum epochs reached).
- Mean Accuracy: 61.550%.
- Mean Recall: 56.420%.
- Mean Precision: 64.115%.

Conclusions:
- Dropout proved to be a highly effective regularization technique for this dense 
  architecture, pushing validation accuracy past 61%.
- Training took considerably longer to converge, reaching the maximum number of epochs 
  without triggering early stopping, which is typical behavior when using high dropout rates.
- The model shows excellent robustness, with training and validation metrics moving 
  almost in tandem throughout the first 60 epochs.
