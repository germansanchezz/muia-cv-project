Experiment 01: L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)

Hypothesis:
- Test whether adding L2 regularization (penalty of 0.001) to all hidden dense 
  layers reduces the severe overfitting observed in deep feedforward networks by 
  constraining the size of the weights.

Kept equal to baseline:
- Original, unnormalized image inputs.
- SGD with learning rate 3e-4, momentum=0.9 and nesterov=True.
- Batch size: 16.
- Maximum epochs: 100.
- Same train/validation split and callbacks.

Changed:
- Architecture changed to include L2 regularization:
  Flatten -> Dense(512, L2) -> Dense(256, L2) -> Dense(128, L2) -> Dense(64, L2) -> Dense(13).
- Checkpoint and prediction paths point to experiment_01.

Challenge compliance:
- Feedforward neural network using dense layers only.
- No CNN, transfer learning, or classical data augmentation.
- USES norm penalties (L2 Regularization), which is the focus of this experiment.

Results:
- Best validation model: epoch 68.
- Training stopped at epoch 98 due to the early-stopping criterion.
- Mean Accuracy: 59.241%.
- Mean Recall: 53.112%.
- Mean Precision: 62.834%.

Conclusions:
- Adding L2 regularization produced a solid accuracy improvement over the unregularized 
  baseline, jumping to ~59.2%.
- The gap between training accuracy (~71.5%) and validation accuracy narrowed significantly, 
  proving that weight decay effectively mitigated the extreme overfitting seen in previous runs.
- The network required more epochs to reach its peak performance, as the weight penalty 
  slows down the capacity to memorize the training set.
