Experiment 10: one additional dense layer

Hypothesis:
- Test whether adding a Dense(64) layer before the output improves the
  experiment_09 ffNN while preserving its successful optimization settings.

Kept equal to experiment_09:
- Original, unnormalized image inputs.
- SGD with learning rate 3e-4, momentum=0.9 and nesterov=True.
- Batch size: 16.
- Maximum epochs: 100.
- Same train/validation split and callbacks.

Changed:
- Architecture changed from
  Flatten -> Dense(512) -> Dense(256) -> Dense(128) -> Dense(13)
  to
  Flatten -> Dense(512) -> Dense(256) -> Dense(128) -> Dense(64) -> Dense(13).
- Checkpoint and prediction paths point to experiment_10.

Challenge compliance:
- Feedforward neural network using dense layers only.
- No CNN, transfer learning, data augmentation, dropout, batch normalization,
  class weights or norm penalties.

Results:
- Best validation model: epoch 54.
- Training stopped at epoch 94 due to the early-stopping criterion.
- Mean Accuracy: 55.947%.
- Mean Recall: 49.268%.
- Mean Precision: 60.728%.

Conclusions:
- Adding a Dense(64) layer produced a small accuracy improvement over experiment_09, from 55.733% to 55.947%, equivalent to approximately 0.21 
  percentage points.
- Mean Precision increased from 53.474% to 60.728%, while Mean Recall decreased from 50.048% to 49.268%. The additional layer therefore did not 
  improve all metrics.
- At the best validation epoch, training accuracy was approximately 82.05%, compared with 55.947% validation accuracy, indicating substantial 
  overfitting.
- This experiment achieved the highest recorded validation accuracy in the non-normalized experiment line. However, the improvement over
  experiment_09 amounts to only four additional correct predictions among 1,875 validation images and should be checked across repeated runs 
  before concluding that the deeper architecture is consistently better.
