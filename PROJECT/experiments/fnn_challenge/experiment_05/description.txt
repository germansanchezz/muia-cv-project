Experiment 05: deeper ffNN with larger batch and longer training

Architecture:
- Flatten(input_shape=(224, 224, 3)).
- Dense(512, activation="relu").
- Dense(256, activation="relu").
- Dense(128, activation="relu").
- Dense(13, activation="softmax").

Training configuration:
- Adam optimizer.
- Learning rate: 1e-4.
- beta_1=0.9, beta_2=0.999.
- amsgrad=True.
- clipnorm=1.0.
- Batch size: 32.
- Maximum epochs: 100.
- ReduceLROnPlateau on val_accuracy, factor=0.1, patience=10.
- EarlyStopping on val_accuracy, patience=40.
- ModelCheckpoint saving the best val_accuracy model.
- Images were not normalized in this experiment.

Challenge compliance:
- This experiment uses only a feedforward neural network made of dense layers.
- No CNN, transfer learning, data augmentation, dropout, batch normalization or
  norm penalties were used.
- The changes are limited to architecture and training parameters allowed in
  this challenge phase.

Results:
- Best validation model: epoch 46.
- Training stopped at epoch 86 due to the early-stopping criterion.
- Mean Accuracy: 47.573%.
- Mean Recall: 40.211%.
- Mean Precision: 42.761%.

Class-level observations:
- Building recall was 63.361%, lower than in experiment_04, while precision
  increased to 56.098%.
- Small car remained strong with 79.027% recall.
- Cargo plane, Fishing vessel, Excavator, Shipping container and Pylon showed
  meaningful recall improvements compared with earlier experiments.
- Helipad remained the weakest class, with 0.000% recall.

Conclusions:
- This is the best configuration tested so far for the main Mean Accuracy
  metric, improving substantially over experiment_04.
- Increasing the ffNN capacity together with a larger batch size, a lower
  learning rate and a longer training budget was more effective than simply
  adding a small hidden layer.
- The model still overfits to some extent: training accuracy reached about 56%,
  while the best validation accuracy was 47.573%.

Next experiment:
- Experiment_06 will keep this architecture and training configuration, and
  will add input normalization to test whether scaling the inputs can improve
  the current best result.
